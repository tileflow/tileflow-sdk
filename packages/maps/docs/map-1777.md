# 1777

`map1777` adapts the ink and watercolor vocabulary of the 1777 cabinet atlas to current
Tileflow World data. Pale cream paper, vermilion buildings, green landscape washes, fine
agricultural strokes and serif lettering carry the style. 1777 describes the visual reference,
not historical coverage.

The reference is the large-scale manuscript cabinet atlas held by KBR. The visual study
compared Namur, Uccle/Sonian Forest, Dilbeek, Mon Plaisir, Park Abbey and Sart-Bernard:
urban blocks, estate gardens, open farmland and woodland need different marks.

Mapped fields use three wash colors and three fine hatch directions, selected by feature ID;
features without IDs use the first plate (including the current World landcover tiles). This changes the surface treatment within existing
polygons, without drawing invented parcel boundaries. Woods and gardens use subtle pigment grain and fibers; their textures contain no tree
silhouettes or repeated orchard dots. Modern residential zoning has a quiet fiber texture,
so decorative rectangles do not compete with actual footprints. Study of the Brussels and Namur sheets informs the finer building divisions, pale water interiors and softly pooled bank pigment.
Building outlines become more distinct as the camera approaches, allowing red blocks to read
together at neighborhood scale. Opaque water covers shared bank edges at river confluences
and its survey centerlines; small streams
without polygons keep their ink strokes and intermittent marks. Ferry routes remain as fine,
subdued dashes. Water names use dark italic ink, a soft water-colored halo and wider repetition
spacing. Roads use ivory paper-colored interiors and fine brown borders, following the
[Cabinet Map detail published by KBR](https://www.kbr.be/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/detail-van-de-kabinetskaart.png).
Width carries the hierarchy; major roads do not use an ochre fill. Dawn and dusk warm the
ivory, while night uses a legible cool grey-blue.

At regional and city scales, mapped residential areas use a pale red wash to distinguish
settlements from open country. This wash transitions to the detailed paper ground between
zoom 12 and 14 as individual buildings appear. Minor street and tertiary road borders enter
gradually over their first two zoom levels. Peak and aerodrome names start at zoom 12 and 13,
respectively, leaving settlement names clear at regional scales.

The packaged Cormorant Garamond Regular, SemiBold and Italic faces replace the former sans-serif
lettering. Major settlements use spaced capitals; local names, roads and water use mixed-case
italics. These are contemporary licensed fonts, not recovered historical lettering.

In the SDK checkout, preview the source with:

```sh
node packages/cli/dist/index.js preview --config packages/maps/map-1777.config.ts
```

Use **3D** at street scale to raise the buildings and trees. Buildings retain their red
pigment, with fine ink-colored edges, softened corners and contact shading. Trees and
buildings share cast shadows. Light direction stays attached to the map, independent of the camera.

The map offers one **Default** palette family with four artistic times of day:

| Preset | Paper and pigment                                        | Light                                  |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Dawn   | Rose parchment, coral red roofs and sage olives          | Low warm eastern sun                   |
| Day    | Pale laid paper, vermilion roofs and green washes        | Warm southwestern sun                  |
| Dusk   | Amber parchment, russet roofs and dark plum-brown ink    | Low amber western sun and cool shade   |
| Night  | Indigo paper, muted rose-slate roofs and ivory lettering | Cool moonlight with restrained shadows |

Each preset recolors the paper, water and landscape patterns as well as the map
and 3D geometry. There are no Faded or Monochrome families. These presets are fixed artistic
lighting setups; they do not follow the clock. The `light` theme ID remains the Default family
and its Day preset. For a prepared map, select `{theme: 'light', timeOfDay: 'dawn'}` (or `day`,
`dusk`, `night`). Concrete theme IDs are `dawn`, `light`, `dusk`, and `dark`, respectively;
system appearance selects Day or Night.

Pattern SVG variants are package-owned assets. After changing their pigments or source plates,
regenerate them from the SDK checkout with
`pnpm exec tsx packages/maps/scripts/generate-map-1777-patterns.ts`.

The overhead view retains red footprints and small vector circles for mapped trees. Vegetation uses `auto` mode:
the explicit 3D view enables olive trees with illustrated shading, while 2D views and
renderers without model support retain flat trees. Capture and Static Maps use that flat
vegetation fallback. Core's custom building and shadow renderer requires WebGL2; a native
extrusion fallback does not reproduce its shadows.

Pedestrian plazas sit above residential zoning and below gardens and woodland. Broad road
and pier polygons stay hidden so they cannot cover those surfaces. Street and path widths grow smoothly through zoom 22 using Streets' exponential progression,
with carriageways 15% slimmer and fine ink borders. Bridge casings have square ends, tunnels have continuous interiors,
and access restrictions and covered segments preserve the road's continuity. Tunnel dashes
use a narrow border around a continuous interior. Paths use pale gravel with fine ink edges;
pedestrian streets have a wider cream surface. Green garden washes stay distinct from the paper
in all four palettes. Modern street lights and invented night windows
remain off. Street names follow the ground plane and pass behind raised buildings; place
names stay above the city.

Construction carriageways use separate dashed ink plates below roads in service, keeping
ordinary road borders crisp. Highway links draw before receiving carriageways within their
surface or bridge level, with the same widths and access treatments as the adjoining roads.
Major road names take priority over minor streets and service ways. Land-cover and land-use
washes, paths and flat tree symbols fade in over one zoom level; their full-detail pigments
remain consistent across zoom levels.
